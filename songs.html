Songs

the catalog, album by album, in our own words — no lyrics reprinted

BLEACH · 1989

Blew

The opener. A slow, heavy riff dropped low, the first thing the world heard from them.

About a Girl

Written after a breakup. Kurt worried it sounded too pop. Recorded at Reciprocal Recording (Jack Endino, Dec 1988/Jan 1989) and reopened MTV Unplugged in 1993. Listening Room → · Tab →

Negative Creep

A punk blast of self-loathing, sung from the floor. One of the rawest things on the record.

Love Buzz

A cover of the Dutch band Shocking Blue, released as their very first single in 1988.

NEVERMIND · 1991

Smells Like Teen Spirit

The song that started everything. The title came from a joke — a friend sprayed "Kurt Smells Like Teen Spirit" on his wall, Teen Spirit being a deodorant.

Come As You Are

Slow, with a guitar riff you can't forget. "Come as you are" — acceptance, no conditions.

Lithium

Named after the drug prescribed for bipolar disorder. A song about mood swings — one line "I'm so happy," the next "I'm so lonely."

In Bloom

About fans who sang along without understanding what the songs were about. It frustrated Kurt to be heard as background music.

Polly

A quiet, difficult song based on a real news story Kurt read. It is told from inside a situation no one wanted to look at, and it never raises its voice.

Drain You

A love song in Kurt's own strange key — recorded at Sound City Studios (May-June 1991, Butch Vig) with rubber-duck studio noises in the bridge. He called it one of the songs he was proudest of. Listening Room →

Breed

Two minutes of speed and lust. It started life with a different, ruder title and never slowed down.

On a Plain

Self-doubt set to a bright melody — Kurt openly admitted he was not sure what it was about, which is the point.

Something in the Way

The quiet closer: about sleeping rough, under a bridge, in the cold. Kurt said it was drawn from his own past.


the Young Street Bridge in Aberdeen — the bridge from the song

Territorial Pissings

A minute-fifty of pure punk anger, one of the loudest statements on the album.

INCESTICIDE · 1992

Sliver

A loud, simple song about being left at his grandparents' house as a child. Raw and almost funny, and lonely too.

Aneurysm

A fierce live favorite; people argue about the exact meaning, which is part of its pull.

Been a Son

A sharp little song about a family that wanted a son instead of a daughter. Short, bitter, catchy.

Dive

Heavy and sludgy — one of the songs where the band sounds most like their live selves.

Molly's Lips

A cover of the Scottish band The Vaselines, who Kurt adored and championed for years.

IN UTERO · 1993

Serve the Servants

The opening track, and a direct answer to everyone who wanted a version of Kurt that was easier to handle.

Scentless Apprentice

Inspired by the novel Perfume by Patrick Süskind — about a person with no scent of his own. One of the band's ugliest, best recordings.

Heart-Shaped Box

Widely read as being about Courtney Love, though Kurt gave vague answers. Dark, heavy, tense in the chorus.

Rape Me

Against violence — and against the way the press and the industry invaded his life. The title is meant to be brutal.

Frances Farmer Will Have Her Revenge on Seattle

About the actress Frances Farmer, who was broken by the system and locked away. Kurt felt a kinship with her story.

Dumb

About feeling "not right" and numbing yourself. Quiet, like a lullaby, and sad.

Very Ape

A sarcastic self-portrait of a big shot who is really just an ape. Ninety seconds, all teeth.

Milk It

Abrasive, queasy, full of body-horror images. The harshest corner of the album, on purpose.

Pennyroyal Tea

A dark joke about stomach pain: pennyroyal is an herb with a grim history, and Kurt sang about how physically bad he felt.

All Apologies

The closing song. Kurt called it a song for his wife and daughter: "everything I have to say is sorry." Many hear it as a goodbye.

MTV UNPLUGGED · 1994

The Man Who Sold the World

A David Bowie cover, made so completely their own that many people think Nirvana wrote it.

Plateau · Oh Me · Lake of Fire

Three songs by the Meat Puppets, played with their members on stage. Quiet, strange, warm.

Where Did You Sleep Last Night

A folk song from Lead Belly. The last track on the album — and one of the most haunting recordings Kurt left behind.

THE LAST SESSIONS

You Know You're Right

The last song Nirvana recorded, in January 1994. It sat in a vault for eight years, released in 2002 — and sounded like a band at full power.

Oh, the Guilt · 1993

A split single with the Jesus Lizard, from the same sessions as In Utero. Nasty, crawling, alive.

Curmudgeon · 1992

The B-side of "Lithium". Two and a half minutes of pure spite — which is, honestly, what B-sides were for.

Do Re Mi · 1994

A home demo, one of the last things he recorded. Quiet and broken, it surfaced later on With the Lights Out. People argue about whether it sounds like a goodbye.
