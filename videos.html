The Music Videos (1991–1993)

before YouTube, you waited all night by the TV for MTV to play one of these

Kurt did not trust MTV, yet Nirvana made some of the most unforgettable music videos of the 1990s. He wrote treatments in his notebooks, argued with directors over lighting, and turned corporate promo clips into dark jokes that somehow aired every hour.

1. Smells Like Teen Spirit (Sept 1991)

Director: Samuel Bayer · Budget: ~$30,000–$50,000

Shot on a soundstage in Culver City dressed like a bleak high school pep rally. The extras were real local kids recruited at a Roxy show. After hours of sitting in the bleachers in hot smoke, Kurt convinced the director to let them actually mosh — so the riot at the end of the video is real. The cheerleaders with anarchy symbols and the dancing janitor became instant 1991 icons.

2. Come As You Are (Feb 1992)

Director: Kevin Kerslake

Kurt wanted something darker and blurrier — inspired by the Nevermind cover and old German expressionist film. Rain pours over the band, Kurt swings on a chandelier, a dog wears a cone, and a baby-faced swim-through echoes the album art. MTV put it in heavy rotation immediately.

3. Lithium (July 1992)

Director: Kevin Kerslake

Originally meant to be animated, then switched to live footage from the 1991 Paramount Theatre show and tour clips. Kurt hated how standard tour videos looked, so Kerslake cut the quiet verses to calm footage and the loud choruses to pure stage chaos.

4. In Bloom (Nov 1992)

Director: Kevin Kerslake

Nirvana dressed in 1960s suits and horn-rimmed glasses, performing on a fake black-and-white variety show like The Ed Sullivan Show, complete with screaming teenage girls in the audience. Halfway through, they put on dresses and smash the set. It won Best Alternative Video at the 1993 MTV VMAs.

5. Sliver (March 1993)

Director: Kevin Kerslake

Shot on Super 8 film in Kurt’s own garage/basement in Seattle for almost no money. Baby Frances Bean appears in it, dancing toys hang from the ceiling, and a poster of the In Utero anatomical angel hangs on the wall months before the album came out.

6. Heart-Shaped Box (Aug 1993)

Director: Anton Corbijn

Kurt wrote the treatment himself: a dying old man (played by Los Angeles actor and jazz club owner Rex Rose) climbs onto a cross in a field of crows, a girl in a white KKK-style hood reaches for fetuses in a tree, and the saturated Technicolor palette nods directly to The Wizard of Oz. Anton Corbijn brought it to life in two days. It won Best Alternative Video and Best Art Direction at the 1994 MTV VMAs — five months after Kurt died.
