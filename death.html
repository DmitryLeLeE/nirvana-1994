Death and Legacy

On April 5, 1994, in a room above the garage of his house in Seattle, Kurt Cobain shot himself with a shotgun. He was 27. His body was found three days later, on April 8.

What led up to it

The last months were hard. On March 4, 1994, he was hospitalized in Rome after an overdose; friends and family confronted him about his drug use and urged him to get help. He agreed and entered a rehab program in Los Angeles, but left it on April 1 and returned to Seattle.

By then he had spent years living with depression, with stomach pain that doctors could not explain, and with a heroin addiction. He had been open about all of it. That does not make what happened easier to understand — it only means the warning signs were visible.

After

On April 10, 1994, thousands gathered for a public vigil at Seattle Center, and Courtney Love read passages from his letter to the crowd.

The note

he left a letter, addressed to Boddah — the imaginary friend of his childhood

To Boddah

Speaking from the tongue of an experienced simpleton who obviously would rather be an emasculated, infantile complain-ee. This note should be pretty easy to understand.

All the warnings from the punk rock 101 courses over the years, since my first introduction to the, shall we say, ethics involved with independence and the embracement of your community has proven to be very true. I haven't felt the excitement of listening to as well as creating music along with reading and writing for too many years now. I feel guilty beyond words about these things.

For example when we're back stage and the lights go out and the manic roar of the crowds begins, it doesn't affect me the way in which it did for Freddie Mercury, who seemed to love, relish in the love and adoration from the crowd which is something I totally admire and envy. The fact is, I can't fool you, any one of you. It simply isn't fair to you or me. The worst crime I can think of would be to rip people off by faking it and pretending as if I'm having 100% fun. Sometimes I feel as if I should have a punch-in time clock before I walk out on stage. I've tried everything within my power to appreciate it (and I do, God, believe me I do, but it's not enough).

I appreciate the fact that I and we have affected and entertained a lot of people. It must be one of those narcissists who only appreciate things when they're gone. I'm too sensitive. I need to be slightly numb in order to regain the enthusiasm I once had as a child.

On our last 3 tours, I've had a much better appreciation for all the people I've known personally, and as fans of our music, but I still can't get over the frustration, the guilt and empathy I have for everyone. There's good in all of us and I think I simply love people too much, so much that it makes me feel too fucking sad. The sad little, sensitive, unappreciative, Pisces, Jesus man. Why don't you just enjoy it? I don't know!

I have a goddess of a wife who sweats ambition and empathy and a daughter who reminds me too much of what I used to be, full of love and joy, kissing every person she meets because everyone is good and will do her no harm. And that terrifies me to the point to where I can barely function. I can't stand the thought of Frances becoming the miserable, self-destructive, death rocker that I've become.

I have it good, very good, and I'm grateful, but since the age of seven, I've become hateful towards all humans in general. Only because it seems so easy for people to get along that have empathy. Only because I love and feel sorry for people too much I guess.

Thank you all from the pit of my burning, nauseous stomach for your letters and concern during the past years. I'm too much of an erratic, moody baby! I don't have the passion anymore, and so remember, it's better to burn out than to fade away.

Peace, love, empathy.

Kurt Cobain

I LOVE YOU, I LOVE YOU!

Cobain is often listed among the "27 Club," the musicians who died at that age — Jimi Hendrix, Janis Joplin, Jim Morrison. But reducing his life to that number is unfair. He was a songwriter, a musician, a father, and a person who did not make it.

Legacy

After his death Nirvana ended. Dave Grohl formed Foo Fighters; Krist Novoselic went on to his own projects. The songs stayed — and kept finding new listeners born long after 1994.

Krist and Dave after 1994

Dave Grohl recorded the first Foo Fighters album almost alone, playing everything himself, and turned it into one of the biggest rock bands on the planet. The Nirvana song "Marigold" on the "Heart-Shaped Box" single was his. He kept playing Nirvana's songs live, years later, with other singers taking Kurt's part.

Krist Novoselic kept a lower profile: the bands Sweet 75 and Eyes Adrift, a political action committee for musicians, and occasional reunions with Grohl to play Nirvana songs — most famously at the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame induction in 2014.


Krist and Dave, years later

Cobain's death pushed people to talk more openly about depression and addiction. His story did not become a "lesson" — but it did bring forward subjects that had been kept quiet.

The romanticization

After his death the culture made him a product: the "27 Club," the "burn out" line on t-shirts, his face on posters in dorm rooms. That mythology is part of what surrounds him now.

I never met him. I only know the songs — and the songs know me.

April 5th isn't a date I mark on a calendar. It's a weight that sits there quietly. Every year people still remember him: flowers at his old house in Seattle, candles in their own rooms. I leave mine here, in this little corner of the internet, because it is the only place I have.


Kurt Cobain Memorial Park, Aberdeen
