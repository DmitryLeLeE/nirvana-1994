Did you know?

twenty-five things, in no particular order

  • "Smells Like Teen Spirit" is named after a deodorant — Kathleen Hanna spray-painted "Kurt Smells Like Teen Spirit" on his wall, and he thought it sounded cool.
  • Bleach was recorded for about six hundred dollars.
  • Kurt wrote "About a Girl" after spending a whole day listening to Meet the Beatles!.
  • Kurt was left-handed and played left-handed guitars, which he bought cheap in pawnshops.
  • Nevermind was almost called Sheep.
  • The baby on the Nevermind cover, Spencer Elden, grew up and re-created the cover as an adult.
  • "Smells Like Teen Spirit" peaked at number 6 on the Billboard Hot 100 — Nirvana never had a number-one single.
  • Dave Grohl joined in 1990 from the Washington, D.C. band Scream.
  • The working title of In Utero was I Hate Myself and Want to Die — a joke that the label didn't find funny.
  • At the 1992 MTV Video Music Awards, MTV told Nirvana not to play "Rape Me"; Kurt played the opening chords anyway.
  • The Unplugged stage was dressed with candles and lilies because Kurt wanted it to look like a funeral.
  • Kurt's favorite band was the Melvins — he roadied for them and auditioned for them.
  • "tourette's" is written with a lowercase t on the album, on purpose.
  • Krist Novoselic is 6 feet 7 inches tall.
  • "Come As You Are" gets its watery guitar sound from an Electro-Harmonix Small Clone chorus pedal.
  • Kurt painted and drew constantly — he designed the In Utero cover himself.
  • The "Heart-Shaped Box" video was directed by Anton Corbijn.
  • "Sappy" was hidden as a secret track on the 1993 compilation No Alternative.
  • The police photograph of Kurt from 1986 was taken after a graffiti arrest in Aberdeen.
  • "Something in the Way" is about sleeping under the Young Street Bridge in Aberdeen, near the Wishkah River.
  • Nirvana's first single, "Love Buzz," was a cover of a song by the Dutch band Shocking Blue.
  • Kurt said he loved The Beatles before he loved punk, and never stopped.
  • The last song Nirvana recorded, "You Know You're Right," stayed unreleased for eight years.
  • Frances Bean Cobain was named after Frances McKee of The Vaselines, a band Kurt adored.
  • The Unplugged set ended with a Lead Belly song that is over a hundred years old — "In the Pines."
  • Nevermind knocked Michael Jackson’s Dangerous from #1 on January 11, 1992. The same week, Garth Brooks was at #3. The chart was different the following week.
  • Butch Vig produced Nevermind in just seven weeks at Sound City Studios in Van Nuys, California — the same studio where Fleetwood Mac recorded Rumours.
  • Kurt wore a Daniel Johnston “Hi, How Are You” t-shirt to the 1992 MTV VMAs. Johnston’s mail-order cassette distribution tripled overnight.
  • The MTV Unplugged taping on November 18, 1993 was Nirvana’s famous acoustic television performance — though the band continued touring into December 1993 and Europe in February–March 1994.
  • Nirvana’s tour van in 1989 was a Dodge cargo van with no heat. The band and crew slept in it in January, in the Pacific Northwest.
  • Steve Albini recorded In Utero in 14 days in February 1993 at Pachyderm Studio. Studio rental was $24,000 and Albini took a flat fee of $100,000, refusing record royalties.
  • The word “nirvana” in Sanskrit means “blowing out” — the extinction of desire and suffering. Kurt liked that definition. He said it was close to his definition of punk rock.
  • Lollapalooza 1992 offered Nirvana the headlining slot. Kurt turned it down. Pearl Jam headlined instead.
  • The Cobain Journals were published in 2002 by Riverhead Books, photocopied directly from Kurt’s handwritten notebooks. They include diary entries, song drafts, shopping lists, and 20 pages of drawings.
  • At the 1992 Reading Festival, Kurt arrived onstage in a wheelchair pushed by Krist, wearing a hospital gown and blond wig. He stood up and played for two hours.
  • Kurt’s K Records tattoo — on his left forearm — referred to Calvin Johnson’s Olympia, Washington label, home of Beat Happening and the lo-fi DIY scene he loved before grunge.
  • The original price of Nirvana’s first Sub Pop single “Love Buzz” / “Big Cheese” (1988) was $3.00 from the Sub Pop Singles Club.
  • Shonen Knife, the Japanese pop-punk band Kurt called “the greatest band in the world,” toured as Nirvana’s support act in Europe in 1991 and in the US in 1993.
  • At the last show Nirvana ever played — Munich, March 1, 1994 — Kurt had severe laryngitis and bronchitis. The power briefly cut out during “Come As You Are,” and the final song was “Heart-Shaped Box.”
  • On early CD pressings of Nevermind, “Endless, Nameless” is hidden ~10 minutes after “Something in the Way” ends. Some later pressings accidentally left it off.
  • In April 1994, fans on the Usenet group alt.music.nirvana were among the first to spread the news of Kurt’s death over 14.4k dial-up modems.
