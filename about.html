The Band

Related: Kurt Cobain · Family · Influences · Timeline

Nirvana came together in Aberdeen, Washington — a logging town about a hundred miles from Seattle. Kurt Cobain met bassist Krist Novoselic through the local punk scene in the mid-1980s, and by 1987 the band existed under the name Nirvana. Kurt chose the name because he wanted something "beautiful and nice," not an aggressive punk word.

History

The early years were cheap and noisy. The first album, Bleach (1989), was recorded in about three sessions for roughly six hundred dollars and released on the independent label Sub Pop, produced by Jack Endino. It put the band on the map of the Seattle scene, but nothing more.

In 1990, after a long search for a steady drummer, Dave Grohl arrived from the Washington, D.C. band Scream. The trio that most people know was complete.

Nevermind (1991), produced by Butch Vig for the major label DGC, was a different story. The single "Smells Like Teen Spirit" and its video exploded: the album climbed to number one on the Billboard chart, pushed Michael Jackson out of the top spot, and pulled the whole grunge movement out of the underground. A band that had been playing garages found itself at the center of an enormous machine.

Cobain struggled with that fame. The next studio album, In Utero (1993), recorded with Steve Albini, was deliberately rawer and harsher — an answer to what Kurt felt was the too-polished sound of Nevermind. In April 1994 Kurt died, and the band ended.
