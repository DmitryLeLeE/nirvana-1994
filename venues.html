Seattle & Olympia Venues (1988–1994)

the rooms with sticky floors, bad PA systems, and $5 cover charges

Before arenas and MTV, Nirvana played college dorms, pizza parlors, and tiny clubs along the I-5 corridor between Olympia and Seattle. Most of those rooms held 150 to 400 people. If you lived in Seattle in 1990, you could see them for the price of a cheap burger.


Young Street Bridge in Aberdeen & Seattle landmarks

The Clubs

VenueCityWhy it mattered
OK Hotel Seattle (Pioneer Square) April 17, 1991: Nirvana debuted “Smells Like Teen Spirit” live here to raise money for a friend’s medical bills. Nobody in the crowd knew the song yet.
The Off Ramp Café Seattle (Eastlake) November 25, 1990: Dave Grohl’s early Seattle show with the band; legendary raw bootleg recording from this tiny room.
Moore Theatre Seattle (Belltown) Played several landmark early shows here, including the 1990 Sub Pop showcase where Nirvana opened for Mudhoney.
RKCNDY Seattle (Alaskan Way) All-ages club on the waterfront where teenagers could actually get in to see Seattle bands without fake IDs.
Evergreen State College (Dorm K208 / The Loft) Olympia, WA 1987–1989: Nirvana’s first real gigs. Played for 20 college kids, beer kegs, and local K Records punks.
Central Tavern Seattle (Pioneer Square) April 1988: Nirvana’s first Seattle gig, with Sub Pop co-founder Jonathan Poneman watching from the bar.

Record Stores & Hangouts

PlaceWhat you found there
Fallout Records (Capitol Hill) Punk 7-inches, photocopied zines, concert flyers stapled to the wall, and local demo cassettes on consignment.
Easy Street Records (West Seattle) Indie vinyl, in-store shows, and the place Pearl Jam and Nirvana fans actually crossed paths in the aisles.
Sub Pop Offices (Terminal Sales Building) Two rooms, stacks of 45s, and Bruce Pavitt & Jonathan Poneman mailing Singles Club records around the world.
The Crocodile Café (Belltown) Opened 1991; Nirvana played secret/benefit sets here; every Seattle musician drank coffee or beer at the back tables.
